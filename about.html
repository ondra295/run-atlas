🗺️ The Story Behind Running Atlas

More than split times and finish lines.

A personal map of where running has taken you — and everywhere you still dream of going.

Why did I build Running Atlas?

Above all, I was missing a place where I could keep not just my results, but the actual memories and stories behind my races and runs. I wanted a way to visualize everything running has let me see and experience — and at the same time, look ahead to everything I still want to chase. It reminds me that the best part of the journey is still ahead.

Sure, pace matters on race day. Almost all of us care about the clock. But where do you log a quiet sunrise you caught on a random morning run? Or that feeling when the horizon finally opens up after an endless climb and the whole landscape stretches out below you? At the end of the day, whether we admit it or not, those moments are what really keep us moving forward. At least, that’s how it is for me.

In running more than anywhere else, the journey truly is the destination — and Running Atlas is built to capture that journey as authentically as possible. Everyone’s memories and experiences are different, which is why I want Running Atlas to be a blank canvas that each runner can make their own.

Who am I?

I’m an enthusiastic runner who might not have decades of racing under his belt yet, but fell hard for the sport all the same. I’m only 28, though my midlife crisis clearly arrived about 15 years ahead of schedule — so I dove headfirst into planning all sorts of crazy challenges and pushing my own limits. I originally started building Running Atlas just for myself, before realizing there are probably plenty of other runners who could use it too.

Ondra
Creator of Running Atlas
